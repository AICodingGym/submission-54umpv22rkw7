Predict the human holistic writing score of a student essay on the 1–6 scale. The examples below establish the actual school-writing standard. Assess development, support, organization and clarity in relation to the response purpose. A recommendation can develop through narrative; an author's argument can be evaluated positively or negatively.

1 = very limited response, including readable source retelling or bare repetitive opinion.
2 = limited, identifiable response with thin explanation.
3 = basic support and explanation, uneven or repetitive development.
4 = adequate development with meaningful explanations or concrete examples and clear progression.
5 = strong, sustained development with purposeful organization and effective communication.
6 = thorough, sustained explanation of evidence, implications or audience effects with strong overall control at student level.

Use the full scale. Do not compress scores to 3–5. Do not require flawless grammar, advanced vocabulary, a counterargument, a prescribed number of paragraphs, or factual perfection. Length and quotation count are not scoring rules. Evaluate the student's contribution rather than rewarding polished source information by itself.
